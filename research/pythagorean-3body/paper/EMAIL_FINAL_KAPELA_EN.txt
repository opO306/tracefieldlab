I have written a draft computer-assisted proof of the final evolution of the Pythagorean (Burrau) three-body problem. The claim is that mass 3 escapes for t >= 59.73 and masses 4 and 5 stay bound. The approach has two parts:

1. A rigorous enclosure for t in [0, 61]. It uses interval arithmetic on dyadic numbers with outward rounding, Taylor steps with remainder bounds, and a Lohner-QR enclosure with a width-weighted pivot, at 128 bits.
2. A check that the enclosures for t in [59.73, 61] satisfy an escape criterion. The criterion is Yoshida's (1972), with the r(0) <= d condition from his 1973 erratum. Two independently written interval checkers did this check.

I could not find a published rigorous proof of this outcome. I searched ADS, zbMATH Open, scite and general web search; I did not search MathSciNet.

I would be grateful for answers to two questions:
1. Do you know of an existing rigorous or computer-assisted proof of this result?
2. If you have a few minutes, do you see an obvious flaw in this approach?

For transparency: the code, independent checkers and reviews were produced with an AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude), working in separate sessions under sealed test plans. I made the design decisions and reviewed the results.
